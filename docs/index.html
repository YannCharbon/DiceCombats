DiceCombats

DiceCombats helps you manage combat during your tabletop RPG sessions, regardless of the game system you use. Build creatures with the fields you need, track initiative and hit points during combat, and let the app handle the rest: damage resistances, combat logs, and automations with other applications.

Download GitHub

What it does

DiceCombats is for game masters who have never found a tool that quite fits their table. Nothing in the app assumes a particular game system.

Custom fields

A creature is built from the fields you choose to add. Ten field types are available: hit points, numbers, text, checkboxes, grids, stats, conditions, colours, and detailed pop-ups. Once a field is configured, you can save it as a custom field and reuse it on any other creature.

Bestiary and player groups

Your creatures and player characters are stored in the same searchable list and can be reused in any combat. You can also organise players into groups, so the whole party can be added to a combat in one click.

Import from aidedd.org

If you play D&D, you can paste the URL of a monster page from aidedd.org and import its stat block directly, whether it uses the 2014 or 2024 format. Spell links open inside the app, so you do not need to leave it during combat.

Live combat

During combat, roll initiative, sort the turn order, and jump directly to any creature in the list. A timer keeps track of the fight, you can keep general notes, and each instance of a creature keeps its own values.

Damage types and resistances

Define the damage types used by your system, then set each creature's resistances, immunities and vulnerabilities. Attack properties such as magical damage are taken into account, along with temporary and negative hit points. When you apply damage, the app calculates the actual amount and shows you the full breakdown before you confirm it.

Combat log

Everything that happens during combat is recorded: damage dealt by each creature, damage taken, the breakdown by damage type, and round-by-round progress. You can review the statistics afterwards and export the session as JSON.

Automations

Combat events can trigger HTTP requests to any external application. Choose a trigger, apply filters if needed, and build a sequence of requests with optional delays.

Import, export and languages

Export your creatures and combats to share them with other game masters, then import them again when needed. The interface is available in English and French on Windows and Android. DiceCombats is free and open source under GPL-3.0.

Custom fields at a glance

Custom fields are at the heart of DiceCombats. A creature does not use a fixed sheet: you add the fields you need and configure them for your game system. Below are all available field types, with their configuration and how they appear during combat.

  1. Choose a field type from the list and add it to the creature. Give it a name, configure it, and move it up or down on the card as needed.
  2. To reuse a field, save it as a custom field. It then appears in the same list and can be added to any other creature. Fields marked as shared are included in your exports.
  3. During combat, each field switches to its in-play view, and every instance of a creature keeps its own values.

Hit points

Current and maximum hit points. You can also define your system's damage types, the creature's resistances, temporary hit points and, if your game uses them, negative hit points.

Numeric

A simple number with an optional maximum and unit. Useful for armour class, mana points, ammunition, or anything else you need to track.

Text

A free-text field, either single-line or multi-line. Useful for notes you want to update during combat.

Checkbox

A list of checkboxes with labels you define. Useful for legendary actions, spell slots, wounds, or exhaustion levels.

Checkbox grid

Checkboxes arranged in rows and columns. Useful for spell slots by level or ammunition by weapon.

Text grid

A small table you can fill in however you like. Useful for attacks with their bonuses and damage, equipment, reactions, or similar structured information.

Stats

A row of labelled values. Useful for D&D's six ability scores or any set of attributes in your system.

Conditions

The conditions used by your game, which you can add or remove during combat. They are displayed directly on the creature card.

Colour

A colour assigned to a specific instance of a creature in combat. For example, match it to the colour of the miniature's base on your table, so you always know which of the four goblins is which.

Detailed pop-up

A button that opens a window containing text, Markdown, templated HTML, or a PDF. Useful for spells, abilities, or any longer description you do not want permanently displayed on the card.

A look at the app

A few screenshots of DiceCombats in use.

The combat view

This is where you spend most of the session. Creature sheets and their custom fields remain visible, while the initiative window floats above them. Start the timer, jump to the creature whose turn it is, and keep your notes at hand.

Applying damage

When applying damage, enter each part of the attack, select the creature dealing it, and review the result before confirming. The calculation breakdown is shown below the final value, so you can always see exactly how it was resolved.

The combat log

Each combat creates a recorded session. You can review damage dealt by each creature, including its highest hit and average damage, the breakdown by damage type, damage taken by each target, and a round-by-round timeline. Sessions can be exported as JSON together with their statistics.

The creature list

All your creatures and player characters are kept in one searchable list, grouped by initial. From here you can import and export creatures and manage your player groups.

External automations

DiceCombats can trigger HTTP requests to any external API. For example, you can play a sound or trigger a lighting effect when a player deals damage, or change the lighting around the table as turns change. Choose a trigger, filter it by attacker or damage type, and send an HTTP request using values from the event. More complex sequences can combine several requests with delays between them. The editor lists all variables available for each trigger.

Experimental Android support

The app uses a responsive layout and is available for Android as an APK. Android support is currently experimental, and some views may still have limitations.

Your data

Your creatures, combats and settings are kept on your device, or on Windows in a folder of your choice. DiceCombats lets you share them with other game masters, and it keeps backups of them for you.

Sharing a combat with its creatures

When you export a combat with Export to file, the file also holds the creatures the combat uses, as they are in your creature list. Whoever imports it gets the whole encounter, ready to run. Export creatures and Export combats save a whole list in one file. In Settings, in the Data section, Export the whole library… also saves your player groups, user custom fields and automations. The access tokens of your automations are left out, and the person who imports the file is reminded to enter them again.

Importing

Import from a file… is available in the creature list, in the combat list and in Settings. It also opens files exported by older versions of DiceCombats. When nothing in the file is already in your library, a short summary asks you to confirm. When some items are already there, each one is shown as New, Up to date, Differs or Name already used. You then choose what to do with it, for example Import as a copy, Replace the local one, Keep the local one or Use the local one instead. The suggested choice is already selected, and Apply to all of this kind saves you from repeating it. Nothing you already have is changed without your choice, and a backup is made before the import.

Backups

DiceCombats backs up your data the first time you open it each day, and before every import, every restore and every copy of your data to another folder. A backup holds your creatures, combats, player groups, user custom fields, automations, combat logs and settings. The ten most recent automatic backups are kept. The ones you make yourself, and those made before an import, a restore or a copy to another folder, are never deleted by the app.

Restoring a backup

In Settings, in the Data section, the most recent backup is shown with a Restore button, and All backups… lists the others with their date, computer and reason. Before restoring, DiceCombats backs up your current data, so you can go back if needed. Save a backup to a file… backs up your data as it is now and saves it where you choose. You can use it, for example, to take your data from an Android device to another one. Restore from a file… brings it back. On Windows, Open the backups folder shows where they are kept.

Using several computers

On Windows, you can keep your data in a folder that OneDrive, Dropbox or another service keeps in sync. You then find the same creatures and combats on each of your computers.

Choosing the data folder

In Settings, in the Data section, Data folder shows where DiceCombats keeps your data. With Choose another folder…, DiceCombats copies your data to the new folder, checks the copy, then restarts and uses it. Your previous data stays where it was until you remove it with Delete the old copy. Back to the default folder takes your data back to where it was at first. A backup is made before your data is copied.

A folder that already holds your data

On your second computer, choose the same folder. DiceCombats sees that it already holds DiceCombats data, shows what is in it and what is on this computer, and asks what you would like to do. Use the folder's data switches to the folder as it is. Replace it with mine backs up the folder's data, then replaces it with yours. Merge adds your data to the folder's, and you choose what to do when an item exists on both sides, as for an import.

One computer at a time

Please open DiceCombats on one computer at a time. If you start it while it is still open on another computer, a screen tells you which one and since when, and you can choose Open anyway or Quit. If another computer changes your data while DiceCombats is open, a message offers to Reload it when you are ready. DiceCombats never reloads by itself, even in the middle of a combat. If you then change an item that was changed on the other computer, DiceCombats asks whether to Reload or to Overwrite. If DiceCombats is closed before you choose, your changes are kept in the data folder, and you are told about them at the next start. Changes to creatures and combats can then be brought back with Import from a file….

Keeping the files on this device

Please set your synchronisation service to always keep the files of the data folder on this device. Otherwise DiceCombats may have to wait for files to download. When it starts, DiceCombats tells you if some files are only in the cloud. It also tells you if the service has made copies of files that two computers changed at the same time.

When the folder cannot be reached

If your data folder is on an external drive or a network drive that is not connected, DiceCombats shows a screen instead of an empty library. You can Try again, Choose another folder… if your data has moved, Open the default folder instead for this session only, or Quit. With the default folder, a message reminds you that your changes are saved there and not in your data folder.

On Android

The data folder cannot be changed on Android. To take your data to another device, use Export the whole library… and Import from a file…, or Save a backup to a file… and Restore from a file….

The Dice ecosystem

DiceCombats is part of Dice, a family of free, open-source apps I write for my own game table and share with other game masters. Each app covers a different part of running a tabletop RPG, and they all follow the same principles: no installer, no account, your data stays on your machine, and they talk to other programs over HTTP.

Dice Atrium

All the Dice apps are gathered in the Atrium.

Visit the Dice Atrium

DiceAudio

Manages the sound at your table: a library of music, ambiences and sound effects, layered scenes that crossfade from one context to another, and scenarios covering a whole session. Its local API can receive requests from DiceCombats automations, for instance to play a sting on a critical hit. You set up the link yourself, in the settings of both apps.

Visit the DiceAudio page

Install

No installer and no account are needed. DiceCombats stores all its data locally on your device, in a folder you can choose on Windows.

  • Windows: Download the ZIP from the latest release, extract it anywhere, and run DiceCombats.exe. You can pin it to the Start menu for easier access.
  • Android: Download and install the APK. The Android build is experimental, and some views may not yet display perfectly.

Build from source

You need the .NET 10 SDK and the MAUI workloads installed. All other dependencies are restored from NuGet.

git clone https://github.com/YannCharbon/DiceCombats.git
dotnet workload install maui-windows maui-android
dotnet publish DiceCombats/DiceCombats.csproj -f net10.0-windows10.0.19041.0 -c Release -p:RuntimeIdentifierOverride=win10-x64 -p:WindowsPackageType=None -p:IncludeAllContentForSelfExtract=true -p:PublishReadyToRun=true -p:IncludeNativeLibrariesForSelfExtract=true

The features, design choices and overall direction of DiceCombats still come from me, as a game master building a tool for other game masters.
